The rain had stopped, leaving the air thick and smelling of wet earth and jasmine. He was meticulously arranging a small bouquet of wildflowers – mostly daisies and buttercups – on the worn, wooden table of a tiny, family-run cafe. The cafe was empty save for the elderly woman behind the counter, quietly wiping down surfaces with a practiced hand. He kept glancing towards the street, a nervous energy radiating from him. He smoothed down his shirt, adjusted the collar, and then, with a deep breath, checked his watch for the tenth time in as many minutes. A single, perfect raindrop clung to a daisy petal, shimmering in the late afternoon light. He smiled, a hopeful, slightly anxious smile, and continued to fuss with the flowers, as if their arrangement held the key to everything.
